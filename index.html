What the lines are, and what to keep in mind

The lines are estimates. The published reports give, for each group, only the mean, the standard deviation (CAASPP), and the share in a few score bands or achievement levels, plus an exact distribution for all students together. Each group's full distribution was recovered as the smoothest bell-shaped curve that reproduces every published number and adds up, across groups, to the published all-student distribution. Between published band edges the curves are interpolation. Method and uncertainty are in METHOD.md.

CAASPP covers every tested public-school junior in California. The SAT figures cover students in the state's graduating class who took the SAT: 63% of juniors in the class of 2020, about 30% from the class of 2023 on. A share "of test-takers" means something different for the two tests. SAT takers who did not report a race (1–10% of takers, rising over time) are left out of both charts.

Grouping by gender uses the male/female rows of the same tables (CAASPP adds a nonbinary row from 2024-25; students with no reported gender, 0–1% in 2021–2024, are left out). Grouping by economic status is available for CAASPP only; the College Board publishes no score distributions by family income or parental education. Filipino students are counted as Asian, as the College Board does. CAASPP grade 11 is matched to the SAT graduating class one year later. The CAASPP scale ran to 2795 (ELA) and 2862 (math) before 2021 and to 2900 after; students pile up at the ceiling, so the top 1–4% cannot be separated.

"CAASPP grade 11 Math — school-mixture estimate" is an alternative estimate of the same math distributions, built from school-level results (one near-normal component per school and student group) and reconciled to the same published statewide figures. It is not the default. It has no gender grouping. Its bounds are much narrower in the upper tail; see METHOD.md, section 11, and data/estimated_*_schoolmix.csv.

Source tables: CDE Smarter Balanced technical reports (Appendix 7) and College Board SAT Suite annual reports for California. Published tables and the estimates behind the charts are in data/.
